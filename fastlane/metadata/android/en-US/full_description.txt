An alarm clock for people who switch alarms off in their sleep.

RiseAndAaaaaaagh! stops only when you actually do something:

• QR code — print a code, stick it up in another room and scan it. The button buys you a quiet walk there: 3 minutes, then 2, then 1. Miss it and it screams again.
• Shake — silent while you shake, screaming the moment you stop. The harder you shake, the faster it fills. Pick a little, Aaaagh or Earthquake.
• Regular — a slider you have to drag all the way. A sleepy tap is not enough.

Waking up
• Sound that grows from a whisper, at the volume you pick.
• Sunrise light: before the sound starts, the screen glows from deep red to warm white and the flashlight brightens where the phone supports it.
• Strobe, with a photosensitivity warning.
• Your own music file, the phone's alarm sound, or the bundled sounds.
• Internet radio: two minutes before the alarm the station starts silently to check that it plays. If it does not answer or stutters, the melody plays instead.

Hard to escape
• The notification has no buttons, so a smartwatch can show the alarm but cannot stop it.
• Volume keys and Back do nothing while it rings.
• If the phone reboots or the app crashes mid-ring, the ring comes back.
• Lost your code? A last-resort shake appears after the third quiet walk.
• Auto-stop after 15, 30 or 60 minutes, so it does not scream all day while you are away.

Alarm health
A checklist of the settings phones use to silence apps — notifications, full-screen alarms, exact alarms, battery limits — with a button to fix each.
